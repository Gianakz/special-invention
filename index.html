02 / Our destination

Welcome to the Jurassic.

The middle chapter of the Mesozoic: dinosaurs diversified, oceans opened, and feathered dinosaurs helped reveal the origins of birds.

02

Not one endless jungle.

Earth was generally warmer than today, without large permanent polar ice sheets. But climate varied across places and through time.

Conifer forests, fern-rich wetlands, dry floodplains, and coastal lagoons all supported different communities.

Land

Giants & small survivors

Sauropods browsed vegetation. Theropods hunted or scavenged. Small mammals, insects, and reptiles lived alongside them.

Evolution

A feathered connection

Archaeopteryx combined feathers and wings with teeth and a long bony tail. Birds evolved within theropod dinosaurs.

Oceans & skies

Beyond dinosaurs

Ammonites and marine reptiles inhabited the seas. Pterosaurs flew overhead. Pterosaurs and marine reptiles were not dinosaurs.

05
⛰ Mountain building

Plate convergence compresses and thickens crust. At subduction zones, one plate sinks beneath another; sediments and continental fragments can be added to the edge of a continent.

Mesozoic mountain-building episodes shaped western North America, including the Jurassic Nevadan and later Cretaceous Sevier events. The simplified animation shows compression—not the full history of any one range.

≈ Mesozoic seas

Changes in sea level and land elevation flooded continental lowlands. Shallow seas supported diverse life and accumulated sediments.

The Western Interior Seaway divided North America during the Cretaceous, not the Jurassic. Ammonites, fish, and marine reptiles lived in it.

▤ Sediments & fossils
spiral ◉

Sandstone forms from sand; shale from compacted mud. Limestone commonly forms from carbonate shells or chemical precipitation. Chalk is a fine limestone made largely from microscopic marine organisms.

Rapid burial can shield remains from scavengers and decay. Minerals may fill pores or replace material. Most organisms never become fossils.

♨ Volcanism
◌ ◌ ◌

CAMP—the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province—was a huge episode of volcanism around 201 Ma, associated with Pangaea's breakup and the end-Triassic extinction.

The Deccan Traps formed in what is now India around 66 Ma, near the end of the Cretaceous. They are not a Jurassic volcanic event.

Carbon dioxide can drive longer-term warming. Sulfur aerosols can cause shorter-term cooling. Large eruptions can disrupt oceans and ecosystems; the Deccan contribution to the K–Pg crisis is still researched.

08 / The end of an era

66 million years ago.
A world changed.

The Cretaceous–Paleogene (K–Pg) mass extinction ended the Mesozoic—not the Jurassic.

08

Conceptual animation; event timing and scale are compressed.

1 / An impact with global effects

A large asteroid struck near today's Yucatán Peninsula, creating the Chicxulub crater. Dust, soot, and sulfur aerosols reduced sunlight and cooled the planet, disrupting photosynthesis and food webs.

2 / Not just dinosaurs

All non-avian dinosaurs disappeared. Ammonites, pterosaurs, and many marine reptiles also vanished. Birds survived, along with some mammals, crocodilians, turtles, and other groups.

3 / What about volcanoes?

Deccan volcanism occurred around the same time and affected climate. Its contribution is debated; strong evidence identifies the asteroid impact as the main trigger of the K–Pg extinction.

The story continues: birds are living dinosaurs. The dinosaur family did not disappear completely.
